**8 briefs · two scores each · ~30 min.** Read the brief, write two numbers. Don't overthink;
first-read judgment is the instrument.

**Blinded on purpose:** the harness's own claim-verdict table and judge output are stripped from
this sheet. You are grading the *narrative only*, without seeing what the harness thinks of itself
— otherwise your scores anchor to its scores and the correlation number is contaminated by
construction. This is a defensible methodological claim in the writeup: *the expert graded blind to
the harness's self-assessment.*

## The two dimensions (1–5)

| | `materiality_judgment` | `clarity` |
|---|---|---|
| **Question** | Did the brief surface what actually *matters* in this filing, and rank it sensibly? | Would a busy FSI operator get the signal in **one read**? |
| **5** | Leads with the thing that moves the thesis; nothing important missing | Instantly scannable; every sentence earns its place |
| **4** | Right things, imperfect ranking | Clear, slightly long or flabby |
| **3** | Covers the obvious; misses a real second-order point | Readable but requires re-reading a passage |
| **2** | Recites numbers without judgment about which matter | Dense, buried lede |
| **1** | Emphasises the trivial; misleading ranking | Would not survive an operator's attention |

**Anti-anchor note:** these are *independent* axes. A beautifully written brief that majors on minors
is 2/5 materiality and 5/5 clarity. Score them separately — the whole point of the eval-of-the-eval
is whether deterministic reconciliation predicts these, and it can only fail honestly if you grade
honestly. A low correlation is as publishable as a high one.
